Usability Protocol (Expert Researchers)

Study goal

Collect expert usability feedback on the Wearable Device Specification Tool (web app) to evaluate UI/UX clarity, content completeness, and how well the tool supports selecting wearable light loggers and optical radiation dosimeters for research studies.

Target participants

Expert researchers who regularly design studies with wearable light loggers / optical radiation dosimeters (or adjacent wearable sensing devices).

Delivery format

Google Forms survey, completed after brief hands-on use of the app.

Estimated time

15–20 minutes.

Moderator instructions (for recruitment email or pre‑survey text)

  1. Ask participants to open the web app.
  2. Ask them to complete the short task list (below) using any realistic study scenario.
  3. Ask them to fill out the Google Form immediately after.

Task list (include in the Google Form intro)

Task 1 — Orientation

  • Read the “Introduction” page, especially the background and the “Use Cases” section.

Task 2 — Create a specification

  • Fill out the “Specification” page with a realistic study scenario (their own or a plausible one).

  • Complete at least one field in each accordion section: General information, Hardware requirements, Data requirements, Other requirements.

Task 3 — Export

  • Download the Word export (and PDF if desired).

Google Forms structure and questions

Use a 1–5 Likert scale where indicated (1 = strongly disagree / very poor, 5 = strongly agree / excellent). Keep optional open-ended responses to minimize burden and make summarization easier.

Section A — Eligibility & context (short)

  1. Primary domain of work (multiple choice)
    • Circadian / sleep research
    • Vision / light exposure research
    • Other wearable sensing research
    • Other (short text)
  2. Years of experience working with wearable light loggers or optical radiation dosimeters (multiple choice)
    • 0–1
    • 2–4
    • 5–9
    • 10+
  3. Typical study duration(s) (checkboxes)
    • < 24 hours
    • ≤ 21 days
    • > 21 days
    • Mixed / varies

Section B — Task completion check (binary)

  1. I completed all three tasks listed above. (Yes/No)
  2. If not, what prevented completion? (short text)

Section C — UI/UX clarity and navigation (Likert)

  1. The overall layout made it easy to find relevant sections.
  2. The accordion structure (General / Hardware / Data / Other) was intuitive.
  3. Field labels and helper text were clear and unambiguous.
  4. Tooltips provided the right amount of guidance.
  5. The bookmarking / shareable URL concept was understandable.
  6. The export buttons were easy to locate and understand.

Section D — Content coverage and relevance (Likert)

  1. The specification fields cover the most important requirements for wearable light logger selection.
  2. The hardware section captures key measurement and form‑factor considerations.
  3. The data section captures key export and timestamp requirements.
  4. The “Use Cases” framing (short‑ / medium‑ / long‑term use) is appropriate for this domain.
  5. The long‑term usability vs. fidelity trade‑off was clearly communicated.

Section E — Output usefulness (Likert)

  1. The exported Word document is suitable for sharing with manufacturers.
  2. The exported output would support device comparison or procurement decisions.
  3. I would use (or recommend) this tool in future study planning.

Section F — Focused, easy‑to‑summarize open responses

  1. Most helpful element of the UI or content (short text, 1–2 sentences).
  2. Biggest gap or missing requirement (short text, 1–2 sentences).
  3. One change that would most improve the tool (short text, 1–2 sentences).
  4. If you are willing, name a specific requirement you often need that was hard to express here. (short text)

Section G — Optional follow‑up

  1. May we contact you for a brief follow‑up? (Yes/No)
  2. Email (optional) (short text, conditional on Yes)

Suggested summary metrics for the paper

  • Completion rate: % who completed all tasks.
  • UI/UX clarity score: mean of Q6–Q11.
  • Content coverage score: mean of Q12–Q16.
  • Output usefulness score: mean of Q17–Q19.
  • Top themes: summarize Q20–Q22 using 2–4 recurring themes.

Notes for analysis (internal)

  • Use descriptive statistics (mean, SD) for the Likert scale blocks.
  • Report the most frequent qualitative themes with short representative quotes.
  • Map open‑text issues to sections (General, Hardware, Data, Other, Export) for quick prioritization.
